# A1340 BLE

Prime power bank (A1340). This page owns the BLE-specific facts of the product; the command payloads (opcodes, TLV tags, scales) are owned by the MQTT page [../mqtt/a1340.md](../mqtt/a1340.md) and are not repeated here. The frame, fragmentation and encryption are on [protocol.md](protocol.md), the negotiation on [handshake.md](handshake.md).

Evidence is the asm of 3.24.3 (`file.dart @0xADDR`, relative to `charging/ble/device/` unless a path is given). Pool lists such as `[0, 0x5e]` are real values. A row that says `no` in the column `on the MQTT page` means the opcode is not mentioned on the linked page; it can still be a command that also goes over MQTT.

Notes on this product:

- The device class mixes in `ZXBleFeature`, so the ZX handshake applies, but the product has its own GATT family and scan value (see the table in [protocol.md](protocol.md#gatt-profile-per-product)); no other product uses it.
- The controller references 41 opcodes that no other controller in `charging/ble/device/` references, in the groups `008a`, `008c`, `0500` to `0525` (with gaps) and `0d00` to `0d21` (nine values); computed over all controller files.
- Negotiation, app side: `A1340AnkerDevice` (class id 8449) has `ZXBleFeature` in its `extends` line and `A1340` is not a registered SDK product code, so `BleHandler` picks `BleHandlerImpl1`, whose `_authDevice` starts the Dart `ZXBleConfer`. Which of the three conferers then runs depends on the capability response, not on the product ([handshake.md](handshake.md#which-implementation-runs-for-a-product)).

#### Direction

both: requests go app to device, the responses (`08xx` in the common map, [handshake.md](handshake.md#opcode-pairs)) come back device to app.

#### Packet framing

[protocol.md](protocol.md#frame-on-ble); the handshake frames carry the flag bits of [protocol.md](protocol.md#opcode-byte-0-flags).

#### Handshake prerequisite

The rows of group `handshake` are the handshake. Which conferer runs (`SecureEcdhConfer`, `EcdhConfer` or `AesConferV1`) is chosen from the capability response, not from the product: [handshake.md](handshake.md#capability-negotiation-response). The other rows are ordinary commands that need a completed handshake for encrypted frames.

#### TLV tags

The handshake payloads are positional bytes or single tags and are not per-product; they are described in [handshake.md](handshake.md#capability-negotiation-response) and [handshake.md](handshake.md#the-three-conferers). The payloads of the WiFi, OTA and status-request rows were not decoded for this page.

#### Example

none: no capture of a handshake exists in this repository.

#### Related API endpoint

MQTT twin and device page: [../mqtt/a1340.md](../mqtt/a1340.md). The handshake itself has no MQTT twin.

#### Since/changed

3.18.0: the same device-class files. 3.24.3: the opcode set of the device-class file(s) is unchanged; the `ProductModel` of A1340 existed in 3.18.0 (see [protocol.md](protocol.md#since-3180)).

#### Known unknowns

none (app side); device side: device test row 97 ([backlog](../docs/DEVICE_TEST_BACKLOG.md)).

BLE is unavailable while the device is on WiFi; see `ble/protocol.md` (community finding, see protocol.md; not asm-traced).

#### Device-tested by

#### Known unknowns

- Rows marked `no` may be commands that are MQTT-only on the cloud side or simply undocumented on the MQTT page; this page does not decide which.
- The payload tags of the 24 sender rows marked `no` and the response handling of the 18 `handleZXProtocolDeviceResponse` rows. — traced 2026-10-20: the 24 sender rows build their TLV maps inline (Smi keys from raw 324, key 0xA2, upward; for example `setBuzzerSwitchReq` `a1340_device_controller.dart @0x33dbfc4`, `setDeviceResetReq` `@0x3c265f8` with raw 324 and raw 330) and none of them has a shape of [protocol.md](protocol.md#controller-send-wrappers); the OTA rows `getOTAState` (`0089`), `getOTAInfo` (`008a`) and `sendSubPackageData` (`008c`) take a `payload` argument instead of the `valueMap` of those shapes; the value expressions were not read. The 18 `handleZXProtocolDeviceResponse` rows (`@0x3b90738` to `@0x3b90cb0`) lie in one response handler that calls `StringExt.getUnifiedMac` and `parseResponse` before its opcode cases; the cases were not decoded.

BLE is unavailable while the device is on WiFi; see `ble/protocol.md` (community finding, see protocol.md; not asm-traced).
